TITLE: Middle-earth — Major Events and Artifacts
SERIES: The Lord of the Rings
COVERAGE: The Second Age, The Hobbit, The Lord of the Rings, and related historical events
SPOILER LEVEL: FULL
CANON BASIS: J.R.R. Tolkien's published Middle-earth writings

OVERVIEW

The central conflict of The Lord of the Rings originates thousands of years before Frodo Baggins begins his journey. The forging of the Rings of Power, Sauron's rise, the failure to destroy the One Ring after his first great defeat, and the Ring's eventual rediscovery determine the course of the War of the Ring.

Many significant artifacts of Middle-earth possess magical or historical importance. Some represent corruption and domination, while others symbolize hope, continuity, and resistance.

THE FORGING OF THE RINGS OF POWER

During the Second Age, Sauron approached the Elven smiths of Eregion in a fair disguise, presenting himself as Annatar, a giver of gifts. He taught and influenced their craft while secretly pursuing a plan to control the powerful Rings they created.

Celebrimbor, the greatest of Eregion's smiths, created the Three Elven Rings without Sauron's direct participation. These rings were Narya, Nenya, and Vilya.

Sauron secretly forged the One Ring in the fires of Mount Doom, also called Orodruin. He invested a significant portion of his own power into the Ring so that it could dominate the bearers of the other Rings of Power.

When the Elves perceived his intentions, they removed their rings. Sauron then waged war against them, seized many Rings of Power, and distributed them among Dwarves and Men.

The Seven Rings held by Dwarves increased their desire for wealth but did not make them obedient servants of Sauron. The Nine Rings given to Men eventually corrupted their bearers into the Nazgûl, servants whose existence was bound to Sauron's power.

THE THREE ELVEN RINGS

Narya, the Ring of Fire, was eventually entrusted to Gandalf by Círdan the Shipwright. It was associated with kindling courage and resisting despair.

Nenya, the Ring of Water, was borne by Galadriel and helped preserve the beauty and character of Lothlórien.

Vilya, the Ring of Air, was borne by Elrond and associated with the preservation of Rivendell.

The Three were not inherently evil, but their power was connected to the continued existence of the One Ring. When the One was destroyed, the power of the Three diminished, contributing to the fading of the Elven realms.

THE LAST ALLIANCE OF ELVES AND MEN

Near the end of the Second Age, Elendil and Gil-galad led the Last Alliance against Sauron. Their armies marched into Mordor and besieged Barad-dûr.

Sauron was defeated after a final confrontation in which Elendil and Gil-galad both perished. Isildur, Elendil's son, cut the One Ring from Sauron's hand using the broken sword Narsil.

Elrond urged Isildur to destroy the Ring, but Isildur kept it as compensation for his losses. This decision allowed Sauron's eventual return.

Isildur was later killed in an Orc attack near the Gladden Fields. The Ring slipped from his finger and disappeared into the Anduin River.

THE REDISCOVERY OF THE ONE RING

Centuries later, Déagol discovered the Ring while fishing. His companion Sméagol murdered him to obtain it.

The Ring profoundly corrupted Sméagol, who became known as Gollum and retreated beneath the Misty Mountains.

During the events of The Hobbit, Bilbo Baggins found the Ring and used its power of invisibility. Gollum later became consumed with recovering it.

Bilbo eventually left the Ring to Frodo, and Gandalf determined its true identity after investigating its history.

THE FELLOWSHIP AND THE WAR OF THE RING

At the Council of Elrond, representatives of the Free Peoples agreed that the One Ring had to be destroyed in the fires of Mount Doom.

Nine companions formed the Fellowship: Frodo, Sam, Merry, Pippin, Aragorn, Boromir, Legolas, Gimli, and Gandalf.

The Fellowship traveled through Moria, where Gandalf confronted a Balrog and fell into the depths. After reaching Lothlórien, the remaining company traveled south until the Fellowship broke apart near Amon Hen.

Boromir died defending Merry and Pippin, who were captured by Orcs. Frodo and Sam continued toward Mordor, eventually accepting Gollum as their guide.

Meanwhile, Aragorn, Legolas, and Gimli pursued the Orcs and became involved in the defense of Rohan. Gandalf returned as Gandalf the White.

THE FALL OF ISENGARD

Saruman had transformed Isengard into a military stronghold and sent armies against Rohan.

Merry and Pippin's encounter with Treebeard helped bring the Ents into the conflict. The Ents assaulted Isengard, destroyed Saruman's machinery, and flooded much of the fortress.

Saruman lost his position as a major military threat, although his final downfall occurred later in the Shire.

THE BATTLE OF THE PELENNOR FIELDS

Sauron's forces besieged Minas Tirith, Gondor's chief city.

The Rohirrim arrived to assist Gondor. During the battle, King Théoden was mortally wounded, and the Witch-king confronted Éowyn.

Merry struck the Witch-king with a blade originally made in the northern kingdom of Arnor, helping break the supernatural protection surrounding him. Éowyn then delivered the fatal blow.

Aragorn arrived aboard captured ships from the south, bringing reinforcements who helped secure Gondor's victory.

THE DESTRUCTION OF THE ONE RING

To distract Sauron from Frodo's mission, Aragorn and his allies marched toward the Black Gate of Mordor.

Frodo and Sam reached Mount Doom, but at the final moment Frodo succumbed to the Ring's influence and claimed it for himself.

Gollum attacked Frodo, bit off the finger bearing the Ring, and reclaimed it. During his celebration, he lost his footing and fell into the fire.

The Ring was destroyed, causing Sauron's power to collapse and his military forces to lose their central direction.

Although Frodo did not willingly surrender the Ring at the final moment, the mercy shown to Gollum earlier in the journey helped make its destruction possible.

IMPORTANT ARTIFACTS

The One Ring is the central artifact of the story. It grants invisibility to many mortal wearers, prolongs their lives unnaturally, and corrupts their desires. Its central purpose is domination through Sauron's invested power.

Narsil was the sword of Elendil, broken during the struggle against Sauron. Its fragments were eventually reforged into Andúril, the sword carried by Aragorn as a symbol of his restored kingship.

Sting is the Elven blade discovered by Bilbo during The Hobbit and later given to Frodo. Its blade glows blue when Orcs are nearby.

Frodo's mithril coat, also given to him by Bilbo, provides extraordinary protection despite its light weight.

The Phial of Galadriel contains light associated with Eärendil's star. It helps Frodo and Sam withstand darkness and confront Shelob.

The palantíri are ancient seeing-stones that allow communication and distant observation. Their use is dangerous when influenced by a more powerful or deceptive mind, as demonstrated by Sauron's manipulation of other users.

THE SCOURING OF THE SHIRE AND THE FOURTH AGE

After Sauron's defeat, the Hobbits returned home to find the Shire oppressed by men serving Saruman. Merry, Pippin, Sam, and Frodo helped free their homeland.

Saruman was eventually killed by his servant Gríma Wormtongue, who was then killed by Hobbit archers.

Aragorn's coronation marked the restoration of the northern and southern kingdoms under one king.

Frodo eventually departed Middle-earth from the Grey Havens with Bilbo, Gandalf, Elrond, and Galadriel, signaling the passing of the Third Age and the beginning of a new era.

CANON NOTES AND LIMITATIONS

The novels differ from film adaptations in several important details. In Tolkien's book, the Army of the Dead assists Aragorn against the Corsairs at Pelargir rather than fighting directly at Minas Tirith.

The Scouring of the Shire is also a significant event in the novel but is largely absent from the theatrical film narrative.

The Rings of Power and the history of Númenor are explored more extensively in Tolkien's wider legendarium. This document summarizes those events as background to The Lord of the Rings without treating television or film adaptations as primary literary canon.
