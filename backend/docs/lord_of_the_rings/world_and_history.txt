TITLE: Middle-earth — World and History
SERIES: The Lord of the Rings
COVERAGE: The Silmarillion, The Hobbit, The Lord of the Rings, and relevant wider legendarium
SPOILER LEVEL: FULL
CANON BASIS: J.R.R. Tolkien's published Middle-earth writings

OVERVIEW

Middle-earth is the central setting of J.R.R. Tolkien's fantasy legendarium. Its history encompasses thousands of years of conflict between powerful beings, kingdoms, and peoples. The events of The Lord of the Rings occur near the end of the Third Age, when the Dark Lord Sauron attempts to reclaim the One Ring and establish lasting dominion over Middle-earth.

Understanding Middle-earth requires examining its creation, the rise and fall of kingdoms, and the long history of the struggle against Morgoth and Sauron.

THE CREATION OF ARDA

According to Tolkien's mythology, Eru Ilúvatar is the supreme creator. He brought the Ainur into existence, and the themes of their great Music formed the design from which the world would emerge.

Some of the Ainur entered the created world, called Eä, to shape and govern Arda. The greatest among these were known as the Valar, while lesser spirits were called the Maiar.

Melkor, later known as Morgoth, rebelled against Ilúvatar's design and sought to dominate creation. His corruption of the world established the central struggle that shaped much of Middle-earth's early history.

Sauron was originally a Maia who entered Morgoth's service and eventually became the primary Dark Lord of the Second and Third Ages.

THE FIRST AGE

The First Age was marked by the conflict between Morgoth and the Elves and their allies in the northwestern region of Middle-earth known as Beleriand.

The Elves established powerful kingdoms, while Morgoth ruled from his fortress of Angband. His servants included Orcs, Balrogs, and dragons.

The Silmarils, three jewels created by the Elf Fëanor, became central to the wars of this period after Morgoth stole them.

The First Age ended with the War of Wrath, when the forces of the West overthrew Morgoth. Much of Beleriand was destroyed or submerged in the aftermath.

Although Morgoth was removed from the world, the evil he had spread remained.

THE SECOND AGE

During the Second Age, Sauron rose to become the dominant enemy of the Free Peoples.

The island kingdom of Númenor was established for Men who had aided the forces opposing Morgoth. Númenor developed into a powerful maritime civilization.

Over time, many Númenóreans became increasingly concerned with mortality and envious of the immortality of the Elves.

Sauron eventually manipulated Númenor's rulers, particularly Ar-Pharazôn, into rebellion against the Valar. The attempted invasion of the Undying Lands resulted in Númenor's destruction.

The survivors who remained faithful, led by Elendil and his sons Isildur and Anárion, established the kingdoms of Arnor and Gondor in Middle-earth.

The Second Age also witnessed the forging of the Rings of Power. Sauron secretly created the One Ring to dominate the bearers of the other rings.

Near the end of the Age, the Last Alliance of Elves and Men defeated Sauron's armies. Gil-galad and Elendil fell in the final struggle with Sauron, and Isildur cut the One Ring from Sauron's hand.

Rather than destroy the Ring, Isildur claimed it, allowing Sauron's eventual return.

THE THIRD AGE

The Third Age saw the gradual decline of many ancient kingdoms.

Arnor fragmented and ultimately fell, while Gondor endured repeated wars and a gradual reduction in its power.

The kingdom of Rohan eventually became Gondor's important northern ally.

Meanwhile, the Elves increasingly withdrew from the affairs of Middle-earth as their time there approached its end.

The Wizards, known as the Istari, arrived to help resist Sauron. Gandalf remained committed to this purpose, while Saruman eventually pursued power for himself.

Sauron gradually recovered his strength, ultimately reestablishing himself in Mordor.

The discovery of the One Ring by Bilbo Baggins during the events of The Hobbit set the stage for the War of the Ring.

THE WAR OF THE RING AND THE FOURTH AGE

In The Lord of the Rings, Frodo Baggins undertakes the mission of carrying the One Ring to Mount Doom, where it can be destroyed.

The Fellowship assists him, while the peoples of Gondor, Rohan, and their allies resist Sauron's armies.

At Mount Doom, Gollum seizes the Ring but falls into the fire, destroying both himself and the Ring. Sauron's power is broken.

Aragorn becomes King Elessar of the Reunited Kingdom of Gondor and Arnor, marking the beginning of a renewed era for the kingdoms of Men.

The Fourth Age represents the growing dominion of humanity and the diminishing presence of Elves and other ancient powers.

IMPORTANT REGIONS AND KINGDOMS

The Shire is the peaceful homeland of the Hobbits in northwestern Middle-earth.

Rivendell is an Elven refuge ruled by Elrond, while Lothlórien is associated with Galadriel and Celeborn.

Gondor is a major southern kingdom descended from Númenórean settlers. Minas Tirith serves as its capital during the War of the Ring.

Rohan is a kingdom famous for its horsemen and its alliance with Gondor.

Mordor is Sauron's stronghold, containing Mount Doom and the fortress of Barad-dûr.

Moria, also called Khazad-dûm, is an ancient Dwarven kingdom beneath the Misty Mountains.

CANON NOTES AND LIMITATIONS

The Lord of the Rings tells only part of Middle-earth's wider history. Many First and Second Age events are described more fully in The Silmarillion and other published Tolkien writings.

Some details of Tolkien's mythology changed during his lifetime, and posthumously published materials may contain alternative accounts.

This document follows the commonly presented narrative of Tolkien's legendarium and distinguishes major historical events from the narrower timeframe of The Lord of the Rings.
