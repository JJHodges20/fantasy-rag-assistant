TITLE: Harry Potter — Hogwarts and the Wizarding World
SERIES: Harry Potter
COVERAGE: Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone through Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows
SPOILER LEVEL: FULL
CANON BASIS: J.K. Rowling's seven original Harry Potter novels

OVERVIEW

The Harry Potter series takes place in a hidden magical society that exists alongside the ordinary nonmagical world. Witches and wizards maintain their own schools, government institutions, laws, businesses, transportation systems, and cultural traditions.

Much of the story takes place at Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry, a magical boarding school in Scotland. Although Hogwarts offers students an education in magic, it also becomes central to the struggle against Lord Voldemort and his followers.

The wizarding world contains significant social divisions, including prejudice based on magical ancestry, disagreements over the treatment of nonhuman magical beings, and conflicts between government authority and individual freedom.

HOGWARTS SCHOOL OF WITCHCRAFT AND WIZARDRY

Hogwarts was founded more than a thousand years before the events of the main series by four powerful witches and wizards: Godric Gryffindor, Helga Hufflepuff, Rowena Ravenclaw, and Salazar Slytherin.

The school is located in an ancient castle protected by powerful magical enchantments. These conceal it from most nonmagical people and help prevent unauthorized access.

Hogwarts normally accepts students beginning around the age of eleven. Students generally attend for seven years, studying subjects such as Charms, Transfiguration, Potions, Herbology, Defense Against the Dark Arts, Astronomy, and History of Magic.

Older students may also study subjects including Care of Magical Creatures, Divination, Arithmancy, Ancient Runes, and Muggle Studies.

Fifth-year students usually take Ordinary Wizarding Levels, commonly called O.W.L.s. Seventh-year students may take Nastily Exhausting Wizarding Tests, or N.E.W.T.s, which help determine access to certain professional careers.

The school is overseen by a headmaster or headmistress, supported by professors and other staff.

THE FOUR HOGWARTS HOUSES

Every student is assigned to one of four houses through a ceremony involving the Sorting Hat.

The Sorting Hat considers students' abilities, personalities, values, and sometimes their personal preferences.

GRYFFINDOR

Gryffindor was founded by Godric Gryffindor. It is traditionally associated with courage, bravery, determination, and chivalry.

Its emblematic animal is the lion, and its house colors are scarlet and gold.

Harry Potter, Hermione Granger, Ron Weasley, Neville Longbottom, and Albus Dumbledore are associated with Gryffindor.

HUFFLEPUFF

Hufflepuff was founded by Helga Hufflepuff. It emphasizes loyalty, patience, fairness, dedication, and hard work.

Its emblematic animal is the badger, and its colors are yellow and black.

Cedric Diggory and Nymphadora Tonks are notable Hufflepuffs.

RAVENCLAW

Ravenclaw was founded by Rowena Ravenclaw. Its traditional values include intelligence, curiosity, wisdom, learning, and creativity.

Its emblematic animal is the eagle, and its colors are blue and bronze in the novels.

Luna Lovegood and Cho Chang are members of Ravenclaw.

SLYTHERIN

Slytherin was founded by Salazar Slytherin. It is associated with ambition, resourcefulness, determination, and cunning.

Its emblematic animal is the serpent, and its colors are green and silver.

Draco Malfoy, Severus Snape, and Tom Riddle, who later becomes Voldemort, belong to Slytherin.

Although several major villains come from Slytherin, membership in the house does not automatically indicate evil intentions.

THE CHAMBER OF SECRETS

Salazar Slytherin disagreed with the other founders about admitting students born to nonmagical families.

Before leaving Hogwarts, he created the Chamber of Secrets beneath the school.

The Chamber housed a Basilisk, an enormous magical serpent capable of killing with its direct gaze.

Slytherin intended the monster to be controlled by his heir and used against students he considered unworthy.

Tom Riddle, a descendant of Slytherin, opened the Chamber during his student years. He later used a magical diary containing a preserved fragment of his soul to manipulate Ginny Weasley into reopening it.

Harry eventually entered the Chamber, defeated the Basilisk, and destroyed Riddle's diary using one of the creature's venomous fangs.

HOGWARTS LOCATIONS

The Great Hall is used for meals, ceremonies, announcements, and important gatherings. Its enchanted ceiling reflects the appearance of the sky outside.

The Hogwarts library contains extensive magical knowledge, including a Restricted Section holding dangerous or advanced texts.

The Forbidden Forest borders the school grounds and is home to creatures including centaurs, unicorns, Acromantulas, and Thestrals.

The Room of Requirement is a magical chamber that appears when someone has a genuine need for it. It can adapt its contents and layout to different purposes.

The Room becomes a meeting place for Dumbledore's Army and later serves as a refuge for students resisting Voldemort's control.

The Astronomy Tower is one of the castle's tallest structures. Albus Dumbledore dies there after Severus Snape casts the Killing Curse.

THE MINISTRY OF MAGIC

The British Ministry of Magic serves as the governing institution for much of the wizarding community in Britain.

It enforces magical law, regulates dangerous activities, maintains secrecy from Muggles, and oversees numerous administrative departments.

The Minister for Magic is the government's leading official.

Important ministries include the Department of Magical Law Enforcement, the Department for the Regulation and Control of Magical Creatures, and the Department of Mysteries.

The Department of Mysteries conducts secretive work involving magical phenomena such as prophecy, time, death, and other fundamental forces.

Ministry personnel called Aurors are specially trained to investigate and capture dangerous Dark wizards.

However, the Ministry is not consistently trustworthy. Throughout the series it demonstrates political cowardice, corruption, manipulation of public information, and discrimination.

Cornelius Fudge initially refuses to accept Voldemort's return because acknowledging the threat would jeopardize political stability and his own position.

Later, Rufus Scrimgeour attempts to respond more directly to the danger but also prioritizes the government's image.

After Voldemort's supporters seize control, the Ministry becomes an instrument of persecution.

THE INTERNATIONAL STATUTE OF SECRECY

The International Statute of Wizarding Secrecy was established in the late seventeenth century to prevent widespread exposure of the magical world.

The statute requires witches and wizards to conceal the existence of magic from ordinary nonmagical society.

Magical governments use concealment enchantments, modified memories, and other methods to maintain secrecy.

The statute contributes to the separation between magical and nonmagical culture, although some wizarding families maintain close relationships with Muggles.

BLOOD STATUS AND SOCIAL PREJUDICE

Pure-blood wizards are generally understood to come from families claiming exclusively magical ancestry.

Half-blood wizards have mixed magical and nonmagical family backgrounds.

Muggle-born witches and wizards possess magical abilities despite being born to nonmagical parents.

Blood status has no reliable connection to a person's intelligence, character, or magical potential.

Nevertheless, some influential wizarding families promote the belief that pure-blood ancestry confers superiority.

Voldemort and his Death Eaters exploit this prejudice to justify persecution.

Hermione Granger, a highly talented Muggle-born witch, directly contradicts claims that magical ability depends upon pure-blood ancestry.

NONHUMAN MAGICAL BEINGS

House-elves are magical beings who often serve wizarding households or institutions. Many are bound to their masters and cannot freely leave their service.

Dobby, a house-elf formerly owned by the Malfoy family, becomes an important ally to Harry after gaining his freedom.

Goblins possess their own language, customs, and traditions. Many work in finance and craftsmanship, including at Gringotts Wizarding Bank.

Goblins and wizards have longstanding disagreements over property ownership, wand rights, and political power.

Centaurs, merpeople, giants, and other magical beings maintain distinct communities, often experiencing difficult or hostile relationships with wizarding authorities.

IMPORTANT WIZARDING LOCATIONS

Diagon Alley is a hidden commercial street in London where witches and wizards purchase books, wands, school supplies, and magical equipment.

Gringotts Wizarding Bank is operated by goblins and contains heavily protected vaults used for storing wealth and valuable objects.

Hogsmeade is a magical village near Hogwarts, containing shops and gathering places such as the Three Broomsticks and the Hog's Head.

Azkaban is a remote prison formerly guarded by Dementors, creatures that drain happiness and can inflict severe psychological suffering.

The Ministry of Magic is headquartered beneath London.

Godric's Hollow is a village associated with several important magical families and historical figures. James and Lily Potter were murdered there by Voldemort.

THE SECOND WIZARDING WAR AND HOGWARTS

After Voldemort returns to power, Hogwarts becomes an increasingly dangerous institution.

Dolores Umbridge attempts to control the school through Ministry authority and suppresses independent Defense Against the Dark Arts education.

Later, after Voldemort takes control of the Ministry, Severus Snape becomes headmaster while the Carrow siblings enforce brutal disciplinary practices.

Students organize resistance through Dumbledore's Army.

The final confrontation occurs during the Battle of Hogwarts, when Harry and his allies defend the castle against Voldemort's forces.

Voldemort is ultimately defeated, bringing the Second Wizarding War to an end.

Following the conflict, Kingsley Shacklebolt becomes Minister for Magic and participates in efforts to reform wizarding government.

CANON NOTES AND LIMITATIONS

This document follows the seven original novels rather than film adaptations.

Some school details, architectural features, and historical explanations differ between novels and films.

The Ministry of Magic described here is the British magical government, not a single government controlling all witches and wizards worldwide.

Hogwarts house values are cultural ideals rather than absolute personality classifications. Individual students often display qualities traditionally associated with multiple houses.
