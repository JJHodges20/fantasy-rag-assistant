TITLE: Narnia — World and History
SERIES: The Chronicles of Narnia
COVERAGE: All seven Chronicles of Narnia novels, including the creation and destruction of Narnia
SPOILER LEVEL: FULL
CANON BASIS: C. S. Lewis's seven original Chronicles of Narnia novels

OVERVIEW

The Chronicles of Narnia takes place across a magical world created by C. S. Lewis. Narnia is inhabited by Talking Beasts, humans, Dwarfs, Giants, Centaurs, Fauns, Dryads, and numerous other extraordinary beings.

Although Narnia frequently resembles a medieval kingdom, its world contains ancient magic, supernatural forces, and pathways connecting it to other realities, including Earth.

The history of Narnia begins with its creation by the great lion Aslan and ends with the destruction of the original Narnian world. Between these events, its inhabitants experience periods of peace, foreign conquest, tyranny, rebellion, exploration, and restoration.

THE CREATION OF NARNIA

The creation of Narnia is described in The Magician's Nephew, which takes place before the events of The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe.

Two children from London, Digory Kirke and Polly Plummer, become involved in magical experiments conducted by Digory's uncle, Andrew Ketterley.

Using enchanted rings, the children discover the Wood between the Worlds, a mysterious place containing pools that provide access to different universes.

One of these worlds is Charn, an ancient, ruined civilization whose final surviving ruler is the witch Jadis.

Digory awakens Jadis, who follows the children back to Earth. During an attempt to return her to another world, the group enters a place of darkness and apparent emptiness.

There they witness Aslan singing Narnia into existence.

His song creates the landscape, stars, plants, animals, mountains, rivers, and forests. Aslan grants speech and understanding to selected animals, establishing the Talking Beasts who become central to Narnian society.

Frank, a London cab driver accidentally brought into the new world, becomes Narnia's first king. His wife Helen joins him and becomes its first queen.

Jadis also enters the new world, establishing the threat that will eventually produce the White Witch's rule.

THE TREE OF PROTECTION

After Narnia's creation, Aslan sends Digory to a distant garden to retrieve a magical apple.

The apple must be planted to grow a protective tree that will help prevent Jadis from invading the young kingdom.

Jadis attempts to tempt Digory into stealing an apple for his seriously ill mother.

Digory chooses to fulfill Aslan's instructions instead.

After he returns, the apple is planted and grows into a protective tree. Aslan subsequently allows Digory to take a fruit from the new tree to heal his mother.

Back on Earth, Digory plants the apple's core. It eventually grows into a tree whose wood is later used to make a wardrobe.

That wardrobe becomes the magical entrance through which Lucy Pevensie first discovers Narnia.

THE WHITE WITCH AND THE LONG WINTER

Many centuries after Narnia's creation, Jadis establishes control over the country and becomes known as the White Witch.

She rules through fear, magical intimidation, and the assistance of creatures loyal to her.

Her reign is associated with an enchanted winter lasting approximately one hundred years.

Under her rule, Narnia becomes a place where resistance is dangerous and many inhabitants must conceal their true loyalties.

The White Witch also fears an ancient prophecy concerning two Sons of Adam and two Daughters of Eve who will occupy the four thrones at Cair Paravel.

The arrival of the Pevensie children ultimately leads to her downfall.

THE ARRIVAL OF THE PEVENSIE CHILDREN

During the Second World War, Peter, Susan, Edmund, and Lucy Pevensie are evacuated from London to the countryside.

They stay in the house of Professor Digory Kirke, who was one of the children present at Narnia's creation.

Lucy enters the wardrobe and discovers Narnia.

She meets Mr. Tumnus, a Faun who initially intends to betray her to the White Witch but ultimately refuses.

Later, Edmund enters Narnia and encounters the White Witch. She tempts him with enchanted Turkish Delight and promises of power.

Eventually all four Pevensie children enter Narnia together.

They discover that Aslan has returned and that a growing resistance is challenging the Witch's rule.

Aslan voluntarily sacrifices himself at the Stone Table to save Edmund from the Witch's claim over him.

A deeper magical law allows Aslan to return to life.

The White Witch is defeated, and the four children become the rulers of Narnia.

THE GOLDEN AGE OF NARNIA

Peter, Susan, Edmund, and Lucy rule together from Cair Paravel, a castle overlooking the sea on Narnia's eastern coast.

Peter becomes High King Peter the Magnificent.

Susan is called Queen Susan the Gentle, Edmund becomes King Edmund the Just, and Lucy becomes Queen Lucy the Valiant.

Their reign is remembered as Narnia's Golden Age, a period of prosperity and relative stability.

The children grow into adults while living in Narnia.

However, when they eventually discover the lamppost near the wardrobe entrance, they return through the wardrobe to Earth.

They emerge as children again, with almost no time having passed in their original world.

This establishes that time does not necessarily progress at the same rate in Narnia and on Earth.

THE EVENTS OF THE HORSE AND HIS BOY

The Horse and His Boy takes place during the Golden Age of the Pevensie rulers.

It follows Shasta, a boy raised in Calormen, and Bree, a Talking Horse who was captured and forced into service.

They attempt to escape north toward Narnia.

Along the journey, they meet Aravis, a young Calormene noblewoman fleeing an unwanted marriage, and her Talking Horse, Hwin.

The travelers discover a plan by Prince Rabadash of Calormen to attack Archenland and threaten Narnia.

Shasta successfully warns King Lune of Archenland, helping prevent the invasion.

He later discovers that his real name is Cor and that he is King Lune's long-lost son and the twin brother of Prince Corin.

Cor and Aravis eventually marry, connecting their personal story to the future history of Archenland.

THE TELMARINE CONQUEST

Long after the Pevensies return to Earth, Narnia is conquered by people known as Telmarines.

The Telmarines descend from humans originally from Earth who entered the Narnian world through a magical passage.

Over generations, they establish a kingdom and suppress the older Narnian inhabitants.

Talking Beasts, Dwarfs, Fauns, and other traditional inhabitants are driven into hiding or treated as legendary creatures.

The Telmarine government discourages belief in Aslan and the earlier history of Narnia.

By the events of Prince Caspian, the kingdom is controlled by King Miraz, who has usurped the throne from the family of his nephew, Prince Caspian.

PRINCE CASPIAN AND THE RESTORATION OF OLD NARNIA

Prince Caspian, later known as Caspian X, learns about Old Narnia from his tutor, Doctor Cornelius.

When Miraz has a son, Caspian's position becomes increasingly dangerous, and he flees the royal court.

He joins the surviving Old Narnians and attempts to restore their freedom.

Caspian uses Queen Susan's magical horn to summon assistance.

Peter, Susan, Edmund, and Lucy return to Narnia, discovering that many centuries have passed since their earlier reign.

They assist Caspian in the conflict against Miraz.

Aslan eventually intervenes, and the Telmarine regime is defeated.

Caspian becomes king and begins restoring peace between humans and the older inhabitants of Narnia.

THE VOYAGE OF THE DAWN TREADER

During The Voyage of the Dawn Treader, Caspian journeys east in search of seven lords who disappeared during Miraz's rule.

He travels aboard the Dawn Treader with Edmund, Lucy, their cousin Eustace Scrubb, and other companions.

The voyage explores remote islands, mysterious magical environments, and regions near the eastern edge of the world.

Eustace is transformed into a dragon after behaving greedily around a dragon's treasure.

Aslan later restores his human form, beginning an important change in Eustace's character.

The courageous Talking Mouse Reepicheep eventually sails toward Aslan's country beyond the eastern sea.

Caspian returns to Narnia and later marries the daughter of Ramandu, a retired star encountered during the voyage.

THE DISAPPEARANCE OF PRINCE RILIAN

In The Silver Chair, Caspian has grown old.

His son, Prince Rilian, has disappeared after searching for the mysterious green serpent responsible for his mother's death.

Aslan sends Eustace Scrubb and Jill Pole to find the missing prince.

They are assisted by Puddleglum, a Marsh-wiggle, and travel north into dangerous territories inhabited by giants and other creatures.

Eventually they discover an underground kingdom ruled by the Lady of the Green Kirtle.

Rilian has been held captive and enchanted, unable to remember his true identity except during a recurring period when he is bound to a silver chair.

The travelers free him and defeat the enchantress.

Rilian returns to Narnia, where he is reunited briefly with Caspian before the old king dies.

THE LAST DAYS OF NARNIA

The Last Battle takes place during the reign of Tirian, Narnia's final king.

An Ape named Shift manipulates a Donkey named Puzzle into wearing a lion skin so that Puzzle can impersonate Aslan.

Shift exploits the false appearance of Aslan to gain power over the Talking Beasts.

His deception becomes connected to an alliance with Calormene forces, whose leaders seek control of Narnia.

The resulting crisis divides the kingdom and undermines trust between its inhabitants.

King Tirian attempts to resist the takeover and receives help from Eustace and Jill.

Despite their efforts, the original kingdom is not restored.

THE END OF THE ORIGINAL NARNIA

During the final events of The Last Battle, the surviving characters pass through a stable door that opens into a reality greater than its physical appearance suggests.

Aslan oversees the end of the original Narnian world.

The stars descend, the land is overwhelmed by destruction, and the old world passes away.

The faithful characters discover Aslan's country, which contains a deeper, more enduring form of Narnia.

The original Narnia is presented as a temporary world, while the true Narnia exists beyond decay and death.

It is revealed that several visitors from Earth have also died in a railway accident and may now remain permanently in Aslan's country.

Susan Pevensie is absent from this final gathering because she has turned away from belief in Narnia. The novels do not establish that her exclusion is necessarily permanent.

IMPORTANT LOCATIONS AND KINGDOMS

Cair Paravel is the royal castle associated with the Pevensies and later Narnian kings.

Lantern Waste is the forested area containing the famous lamppost near the wardrobe entrance.

The Western Wild contains mysterious regions associated with Narnia's earliest history.

Archenland is a human kingdom south of Narnia and traditionally an important ally.

Calormen is a large southern empire ruled by the Tisroc and characterized by a distinct political and religious culture.

Telmar is the ancestral homeland of the Telmarines within the Narnian world.

The Lone Islands are territories associated with Narnia and visited during Caspian's eastern voyage.

Underland is a subterranean realm connected to the events of The Silver Chair.

Aslan's country is a reality beyond the ordinary geography and limitations of Narnia.

CANON NOTES AND LIMITATIONS

The seven books were published in a different order from their internal chronology.

Narnia's passage of time differs from Earth's, and the stories should not be mapped onto a simple one-to-one calendar.

The ending of The Last Battle distinguishes the destruction of the original Narnian world from the continued existence of the true Narnia.

This document follows the seven original novels, not later film, television, or game adaptations.
