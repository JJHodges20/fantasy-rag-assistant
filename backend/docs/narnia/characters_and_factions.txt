TITLE: Narnia — Characters and Factions
SERIES: The Chronicles of Narnia
COVERAGE: All seven Chronicles of Narnia novels
SPOILER LEVEL: FULL
CANON BASIS: C. S. Lewis's seven original Chronicles of Narnia novels

OVERVIEW

The Chronicles of Narnia follows several generations of human rulers, children from Earth, Talking Beasts, and supernatural beings whose lives are connected through the world of Narnia.

Although individual novels focus on different protagonists, the great lion Aslan provides continuity across the entire series. He is present at the world's creation, participates in crucial struggles against evil, and oversees its final transformation.

Characters are frequently judged not by their appearance or social position but by their courage, honesty, loyalty, willingness to change, and response to Aslan.

ASLAN

Aslan is the great lion who creates Narnia and serves as its ultimate authority.

He is described as the son of the Emperor-over-the-Sea, a mysterious supreme figure who is not directly involved in most events.

Aslan is capable of extraordinary supernatural acts, including the creation of life, the transformation of creatures, the breaking of powerful enchantments, and the restoration of life.

He appears in every Narnia novel, although his involvement varies.

Aslan does not function as an ordinary king who permanently resides in a palace. He frequently appears when characters face significant moral or political decisions.

In The Magician's Nephew, Aslan sings Narnia into existence.

In The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe, he sacrifices himself to rescue Edmund from the White Witch's claim and returns to life through deeper magic.

In The Horse and His Boy, Aslan secretly guides Shasta, Aravis, and their companions through danger.

In The Silver Chair, he gives Eustace and Jill signs to guide their search for Prince Rilian.

In The Last Battle, Aslan oversees the ending of the original Narnian world and welcomes characters into his country.

Aslan's role is associated with creation, justice, mercy, redemption, and the possibility of transformation.

DIGORY KIRKE

Digory Kirke is one of the central characters of The Magician's Nephew.

As a child living in London, he becomes involved in the magical experiments of his uncle Andrew Ketterley.

Digory's decision to awaken Jadis in the dead world of Charn eventually introduces the Witch into the newly created Narnia.

He witnesses Aslan creating the world and is given the responsibility of retrieving a magical apple to protect it.

Digory initially hopes to use the apple to heal his gravely ill mother, but ultimately obeys Aslan's instructions.

Aslan later gives him a fruit capable of restoring his mother's health.

Digory grows into Professor Kirke, the elderly scholar whose country house contains the wardrobe used by the Pevensie children.

POLLY PLUMMER

Polly Plummer is Digory's childhood friend and companion in The Magician's Nephew.

She is practical, perceptive, and often more cautious than Digory.

Polly travels with him to the Wood between the Worlds, Charn, and newly created Narnia.

She repeatedly questions reckless decisions and attempts to prevent Digory from taking unnecessary risks.

Polly and Digory remain friends throughout their lives.

Both later appear among the Friends of Narnia in The Last Battle.

ANDREW KETTERLEY

Andrew Ketterley, commonly called Uncle Andrew, is Digory's uncle and an amateur magician.

He creates enchanted rings that permit travel between worlds but uses the children as unwilling participants in his experiments.

Andrew is ambitious, selfish, and frequently unwilling to accept responsibility for the consequences of his actions.

When brought into Narnia, he cannot properly understand the speech of Aslan and the Talking Beasts.

His limited understanding contrasts with the openness and trust shown by Digory, Polly, and Frank the cab driver.

KING FRANK AND QUEEN HELEN

Frank is an ordinary London cab driver who unexpectedly witnesses the creation of Narnia.

Despite lacking aristocratic background or magical knowledge, he demonstrates kindness, humility, and practical good sense.

Aslan appoints Frank as the first King of Narnia.

His wife Helen is brought from Earth and becomes the first Queen.

Their descendants contribute to the royal histories of Narnia and Archenland.

THE PEVENSIE CHILDREN

PETER PEVENSIE

Peter is the eldest of the four Pevensie siblings.

He becomes High King Peter the Magnificent during Narnia's Golden Age.

Peter is associated with leadership, courage, responsibility, and the protection of others.

He helps command Aslan's army against the White Witch's forces.

During Prince Caspian, Peter returns to Narnia and challenges King Miraz to single combat.

After that adventure, Aslan explains that Peter is too old to return to Narnia through further adventures in the ordinary way.

Peter appears again in The Last Battle, among those welcomed into the true Narnia.

SUSAN PEVENSIE

Susan is the second-oldest Pevensie sibling and becomes Queen Susan the Gentle.

She is cautious, practical, and often concerned about the dangers facing her siblings.

During their reign, Susan is known for her beauty, diplomacy, and skill with a bow.

In The Horse and His Boy, Prince Rabadash of Calormen seeks to marry her, creating political danger when she rejects him.

Susan participates in the events of Prince Caspian but does not return to Narnia during the later adventures.

In The Last Battle, the other characters explain that Susan no longer regards Narnia as an important part of her life.

She is absent from the final gathering in Aslan's country.

The novels do not reveal Susan's eventual fate, so her future should not be treated as definitively settled.

EDMUND PEVENSIE

Edmund is the third Pevensie sibling.

During his first visit to Narnia, he becomes vulnerable to the White Witch's manipulation.

She tempts him with enchanted Turkish Delight and promises of political authority.

Edmund betrays his siblings and initially supports the Witch's plans.

After being rescued, he repents and becomes a courageous, dependable companion.

Aslan sacrifices himself to save Edmund from the Witch's claim.

Edmund later becomes King Edmund the Just, known for fairness and mature judgment.

He participates in the events of The Horse and His Boy, Prince Caspian, and The Voyage of the Dawn Treader.

His transformation from betrayal to loyalty is one of the series' central examples of redemption.

LUCY PEVENSIE

Lucy is the youngest Pevensie sibling and the first to discover Narnia.

She enters the wardrobe and meets Mr. Tumnus the Faun.

Lucy possesses a particularly strong sense of trust in Aslan, even when others doubt what she has seen.

She becomes Queen Lucy the Valiant during the Golden Age.

Her gifts include a magical healing cordial and a dagger.

Lucy returns in Prince Caspian and The Voyage of the Dawn Treader.

Throughout the series, she often represents faithfulness, empathy, courage, and the willingness to perceive truths overlooked by others.

MR. TUMNUS

Mr. Tumnus is a Faun living in the snowy woods near Lantern Waste.

He is one of the first Narnian inhabitants Lucy encounters.

Initially, Tumnus is working under the White Witch's orders to capture human visitors.

After befriending Lucy, he refuses to betray her and allows her to return safely.

His actions result in his arrest and transformation into stone.

Aslan later restores Tumnus and other victims of the Witch's enchantments.

Tumnus becomes an important friend and ally of the Pevensie children.

MR. AND MRS. BEAVER

Mr. and Mrs. Beaver are Talking Beasts who help the Pevensie children understand the White Witch's rule and Aslan's return.

They shelter and guide the children, taking them toward Aslan's camp.

The Beavers represent the loyalty of ordinary Narnians who resist tyranny despite personal danger.

JADIS — THE WHITE WITCH

Jadis is originally the ruler of Charn, a civilization destroyed by her use of forbidden magic.

During a war against her sister, Jadis speaks the Deplorable Word, annihilating almost all living beings in Charn.

Digory later awakens her and unintentionally brings her to the world in which Narnia is created.

Jadis eventually becomes the White Witch, who rules Narnia through fear and powerful magic.

She transforms enemies into stone and maintains the enchanted winter.

Her claim over Edmund is based on an ancient magical law concerning traitors.

Aslan defeats her after returning from death.

Jadis is killed during the battle that ends her rule.

PRINCE CASPIAN — CASPIAN X

Caspian X is a Telmarine prince whose uncle Miraz has seized the throne.

Caspian learns about Old Narnia from Doctor Cornelius, his tutor.

He sympathizes with the persecuted Talking Beasts, Dwarfs, Fauns, and other inhabitants.

When Miraz's wife gives birth to a son, Caspian flees to avoid being eliminated as a rival heir.

He becomes the leader of the Old Narnian resistance.

With help from the Pevensies and Aslan, Caspian defeats Miraz's regime and becomes king.

During The Voyage of the Dawn Treader, Caspian travels east to locate seven missing lords.

He eventually marries the daughter of Ramandu, a retired star.

Their son is Prince Rilian.

Caspian dies as an elderly king near the conclusion of The Silver Chair but is restored to youthful life in Aslan's country.

KING MIRAZ AND THE TELMARINES

Miraz is Caspian's uncle and a usurper who controls the Telmarine kingdom.

He is hostile toward Old Narnia and discourages belief in its history.

Miraz becomes especially dangerous to Caspian after the birth of his own son.

During the conflict in Prince Caspian, Peter challenges Miraz to single combat.

Miraz is ultimately killed through treachery by men among his own followers.

The Telmarines are not uniformly evil. Some accept Caspian's rule, while others choose to leave Narnia through a passage opened by Aslan.

DOCTOR CORNELIUS

Doctor Cornelius is Caspian's tutor and a scholar knowledgeable about Narnia's suppressed history.

He secretly teaches Caspian about Aslan, Talking Beasts, and the older kingdom.

Cornelius warns Caspian about Miraz's plans and helps him escape.

His courage is significant because he preserves historical knowledge that the government seeks to erase.

REEPICHEEP

Reepicheep is a Talking Mouse and an exceptionally brave warrior.

He is known for his commitment to honor, courtesy, courage, and loyalty.

Despite his small size, Reepicheep is willing to confront much larger opponents.

He assists Caspian during Prince Caspian and accompanies him aboard the Dawn Treader.

Reepicheep longs to reach Aslan's country beyond the eastern sea.

At the end of The Voyage of the Dawn Treader, he continues his journey alone in a small coracle toward that destination.

His story emphasizes that courage does not depend on physical size.

EUSTACE SCRUBB

Eustace Clarence Scrubb is a cousin of the Pevensie children.

At the beginning of The Voyage of the Dawn Treader, he is selfish, argumentative, and dismissive of imaginative experiences.

He travels to Narnia with Edmund and Lucy and joins Caspian's voyage.

After behaving greedily around a dragon's treasure, Eustace is transformed into a dragon.

His experience forces him to recognize his previous behavior and dependence upon others.

Aslan eventually removes his dragon form and restores him to human shape.

Eustace becomes kinder, braver, and more dependable.

He later returns with Jill Pole in The Silver Chair and The Last Battle.

JILL POLE

Jill Pole is a schoolmate of Eustace Scrubb.

She joins him in Narnia during The Silver Chair.

Aslan gives Jill a series of signs to help guide the search for Prince Rilian.

Jill initially struggles to remember and follow the instructions, resulting in dangerous mistakes.

Over the course of the journey, she develops courage, judgment, and persistence.

Jill returns with Eustace in The Last Battle to assist King Tirian.

PUDDLEGLUM

Puddleglum is a Marsh-wiggle who accompanies Eustace and Jill during their search for Prince Rilian.

He has a gloomy and pessimistic manner, often anticipating the worst possible outcomes.

Despite his apparent pessimism, Puddleglum is extraordinarily loyal and courageous.

When the Lady of the Green Kirtle attempts to convince the travelers that Narnia and Aslan are imaginary, Puddleglum resists her enchantment.

He deliberately stamps on a magical fire, injuring himself while weakening the spell.

His refusal to surrender his beliefs enables the group to escape the Witch's influence.

PRINCE RILIAN

Rilian is the son of Caspian X and the daughter of Ramandu.

His mother is killed by a mysterious green serpent.

While searching for the creature responsible, Rilian disappears.

He falls under the enchantment of the Lady of the Green Kirtle and is imprisoned in an underground kingdom.

Every night, he is bound to a silver chair during a period when his true identity returns.

Eustace, Jill, and Puddleglum eventually free him.

Rilian defeats the enchantress after she transforms into the same serpent that killed his mother.

He returns to Narnia and succeeds his father as king.

THE LADY OF THE GREEN KIRTLE

The Lady of the Green Kirtle is the principal antagonist of The Silver Chair.

She is also associated with a powerful green serpent.

She kills Rilian's mother and then manipulates Rilian into becoming her captive.

Through enchantment, she suppresses his memories and attempts to use him in a plan to conquer Narnia.

She also uses persuasive magic to make the travelers doubt their memories of the surface world and Aslan.

After Puddleglum disrupts her spell, she transforms into a serpent and is killed by Rilian.

The novels do not explicitly establish that she is Jadis resurrected.

SHASTA — PRINCE COR

Shasta is a boy raised in Calormen by a fisherman named Arsheesh.

He learns that he may be sold into slavery and escapes with Bree, a Talking Horse.

During his journey north, Shasta encounters dangers involving Calormene soldiers, deserts, and unfamiliar lands.

He helps expose Prince Rabadash's planned invasion of Archenland.

Shasta eventually discovers that he is actually Cor, the long-lost son of King Lune of Archenland.

He is the twin brother of Prince Corin.

Cor eventually becomes king and marries Aravis.

ARAVIS

Aravis is a young Calormene noblewoman who flees an unwanted marriage.

She travels north with Hwin, a Talking Horse.

Aravis initially displays some of the prejudices and pride associated with her privileged upbringing.

Her journey requires her to depend upon others and reconsider her treatment of people with less power.

She becomes an important companion to Shasta and later marries him.

BREE AND HWIN

Bree is a Talking Horse who was captured and forced to serve in Calormen.

He longs to return to Narnia, where horses can speak and live with greater freedom.

Although Bree is proud of his abilities, he must confront his own fear and limitations.

Hwin is another Talking Horse who accompanies Aravis.

She is often quieter and more cautious than Bree but demonstrates considerable courage.

Both horses represent the importance of freedom and the dangers of treating intelligent beings as property.

KING LUNE AND PRINCE RABADASH

King Lune is the ruler of Archenland and the father of Cor and Corin.

He is an ally of Narnia and attempts to govern with fairness and responsibility.

Prince Rabadash is the son of the Tisroc, ruler of Calormen.

After Queen Susan rejects his marriage proposal, Rabadash attempts to attack Archenland and gain power over Narnia.

He is defeated and later transformed into a donkey by Aslan.

He can recover his human shape by returning to the temple of Tash, but is warned that leaving the temple's vicinity afterward will permanently restore the donkey form.

This restriction helps prevent him from conducting future foreign military campaigns.

KING TIRIAN AND JEWEL

Tirian is the final king of the original Narnia.

He is a courageous ruler who attempts to resist the deception and political takeover described in The Last Battle.

His closest companion is Jewel, a loyal Talking Unicorn.

Tirian's resistance is undermined by betrayal, confusion, and the successful manipulation of many Narnians.

Eustace and Jill arrive to assist him.

Tirian eventually passes through the stable door and enters Aslan's country.

SHIFT AND PUZZLE

Shift is an Ape who becomes the principal architect of the deception in The Last Battle.

He manipulates his companion Puzzle, a Donkey, into wearing a lion skin.

Shift then claims that Puzzle is Aslan and uses the false appearance to control other creatures.

He cooperates with Calormene interests and participates in the exploitation of Narnia.

Puzzle is not the mastermind of the deception. He is frightened, easily manipulated, and eventually recognizes his role in the wrongdoing.

EMETH AND TASH

Emeth is a Calormene soldier who sincerely desires to serve the deity Tash.

During The Last Battle, he enters the stable seeking the being he has worshipped.

Instead, he encounters Aslan.

Aslan accepts the goodness and sincerity of Emeth's actions while distinguishing them from the cruelty associated with Tash.

Tash is portrayed as a genuine, terrifying supernatural being associated with Calormene worship.

The final events distinguish Aslan and Tash rather than presenting them as interchangeable names for the same being.

MAJOR PEOPLES AND FACTIONS

Old Narnians include Talking Beasts, Fauns, Centaurs, Dwarfs, Dryads, and other inhabitants associated with the kingdom's ancient traditions.

Telmarines are human descendants of people who entered the Narnian world from Earth. Their rulers conquered Narnia, although individual Telmarines later support Caspian's restoration.

Calormenes inhabit a large southern empire with its own political institutions and religion. The novels portray both antagonistic Calormene rulers and sympathetic individuals, including Aravis and Emeth.

Archenlanders are humans living in the neighboring kingdom of Archenland, which traditionally maintains friendly relations with Narnia.

The Friends of Narnia are individuals from Earth who have maintained a meaningful connection with Aslan and Narnia. Their final gathering is important to The Last Battle.

CANON NOTES AND LIMITATIONS

The Lady of the Green Kirtle is not explicitly identified as the White Witch in the original novels.

Susan Pevensie's final spiritual fate is left unresolved.

Eustace's dragon transformation changes his character but is not an ordinary permanent magical ability.

The Calormenes should not all be treated as villains. Individual characters have distinct motives and moral choices.

The seven novels span many generations of Narnian history, and characters who share royal titles may belong to different time periods.
