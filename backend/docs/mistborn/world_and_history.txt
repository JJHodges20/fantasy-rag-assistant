TITLE: Mistborn — World and History of Scadrial
SERIES: Mistborn
COVERAGE: The Final Empire, The Well of Ascension, The Hero of Ages, The Alloy of Law, Shadows of Self, The Bands of Mourning, The Lost Metal, and Mistborn: Secret History
SPOILER LEVEL: FULL
CANON BASIS: Brandon Sanderson's published Mistborn novels and relevant Cosmere fiction

OVERVIEW

Mistborn takes place primarily on Scadrial, a planet within Brandon Sanderson's larger fictional universe known as the Cosmere. Scadrial's history is profoundly shaped by two divine powers, Preservation and Ruin, and by the people who attempt to control or resist them.

The original Mistborn trilogy, commonly called Era One, takes place under the thousand-year rule of the Lord Ruler. The second major sequence, called Era Two or the Wax and Wayne series, occurs roughly three centuries after the original trilogy and follows a society transformed by technological development, political reform, and new threats.

Scadrial's history connects magical abilities, religion, political authority, and the balance between creation and destruction.

THE CREATION OF SCADRIAL

Scadrial was created through the cooperation of two powerful entities known as Shards: Preservation and Ruin.

Preservation represented the desire to maintain and protect existing things, while Ruin embodied decay, change, and destruction. The original holders of these powers were Leras and Ati, respectively.

The two powers worked together to create the planet and humanity. Preservation contributed slightly more of its power to human beings, helping establish their capacity for independent thought.

Their cooperation was unstable. Ruin ultimately sought to destroy the world they had created, while Preservation attempted to prevent its destruction.

This conflict became the foundation of Scadrial's mythology and much of its magical history.

THE LORD RULER'S ASCENSION

Before the Final Empire, a young man named Alendi was believed to be the prophesied Hero of Ages.

Rashek, a Terris man accompanying Alendi, learned that allowing Alendi to reach the Well of Ascension could have disastrous consequences.

Rashek killed Alendi and took the power of the Well for himself.

During his brief Ascension, Rashek gained immense knowledge and temporarily acquired the ability to reshape the world.

He moved Scadrial closer to its sun in an attempt to eliminate dangerous mists. This unintentionally increased the planet's temperature.

To compensate, Rashek created volcanic mountains known as Ashmounts. Their eruptions filled the atmosphere with ash, partially blocking sunlight and cooling the surface.

He also altered plants, animals, and human populations to help them survive the resulting environmental conditions.

Although Rashek initially believed he was protecting the world, his changes had devastating consequences.

THE FINAL EMPIRE

Following his Ascension, Rashek established the Final Empire and ruled as the Lord Ruler for approximately one thousand years.

He claimed divine authority and controlled society through political institutions, religious propaganda, military force, and powerful servants.

The empire's capital was Luthadel, a large industrial city dominated by the Lord Ruler's palace, Kredik Shaw.

Society was divided primarily between nobles and skaa. Nobles possessed most political and economic power, while skaa were subjected to exploitation, forced labor, and severe legal restrictions.

The Steel Ministry administered the empire's religious and governmental systems. Its officials included obligators and terrifying Steel Inquisitors.

The Terris people were also heavily controlled because the Lord Ruler feared the combination of their hereditary Feruchemical abilities with Allomancy.

For most ordinary citizens, the world was characterized by ash falling from the sky, unhealthy vegetation, oppressive government, and mysterious mists appearing at night.

KELSIER'S REBELLION

In The Final Empire, Kelsier organizes a crew of thieves, Allomancers, and other specialists to overthrow the Lord Ruler.

Kelsier is a powerful Mistborn who survived imprisonment in the Pits of Hathsin, where his wife Mare died.

He recruits Vin, a young skaa thief with extraordinary Allomantic potential.

The crew infiltrates noble society, organizes rebellion, disrupts economic systems, and attempts to turn the empire's institutions against themselves.

Kelsier is publicly killed by the Lord Ruler, but his death inspires an uprising.

Vin eventually confronts and kills the Lord Ruler by using the mists to help overcome his extraordinary magical powers.

The empire collapses, leaving its territories politically fragmented and vulnerable.

THE WELL OF ASCENSION AND RUIN'S RELEASE

After the Lord Ruler's death, Elend Venture attempts to establish a more representative government.

However, competing armies, political disagreements, and an increasingly unstable environment threaten Luthadel.

Vin begins searching for the Well of Ascension, believing its power might save the world.

The Well actually serves as a prison for Ruin's consciousness.

Ruin has manipulated ancient prophecies and written records to encourage someone to release the Well's power rather than use it.

Vin ultimately releases the power because she believes doing so is morally necessary.

This frees Ruin and allows the destructive Shard to accelerate Scadrial's collapse.

THE HERO OF AGES AND THE CATACENDRE

During The Hero of Ages, earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, ashfalls, and the advancing influence of Ruin threaten all life on Scadrial.

Vin eventually acquires the power of Preservation.

Elend Venture, who has become a Mistborn, leads a desperate military defense against Ruin's forces.

Elend is killed by the Steel Inquisitor Marsh during the final conflict.

Vin then uses Preservation's power to directly attack Ruin, killing both herself and Ati, Ruin's holder.

With both Shards left without holders, Sazed realizes that he is the true Hero of Ages.

Sazed takes up both Preservation and Ruin, combining them into a new divine power called Harmony.

He uses his vast knowledge, preserved through Feruchemy, to repair Scadrial's damaged geography, atmosphere, ecosystems, and orbital conditions.

This transformation is known as the Catacendre.

The world becomes green again, the Ashmounts cease to dominate the landscape, and the surviving population begins rebuilding civilization.

ERA TWO — THE ELENDEL BASIN

Approximately three hundred years after the Catacendre, Scadrial has experienced substantial technological and social development.

The city of Elendel becomes a center of government, commerce, manufacturing, and urban life.

Railroads, firearms, industrial machinery, electricity, and developing transportation technologies replace many aspects of the original empire's economy.

The surrounding Elendel Basin contains fertile land and expanding settlements.

Outside the central cities, the Roughs are less densely governed territories where lawmen confront criminals and political authority is more limited.

Elendel's political institutions are more representative than those of the Final Empire, but wealth, class divisions, corruption, and regional tensions remain important problems.

Waxillium Ladrian, a nobleman and former lawman from the Roughs, becomes a central figure in the conflicts of this era.

SOUTHERN SCADRIAL

The people of Southern Scadrial developed separately from the societies descended from the Final Empire.

The Catacendre dramatically changed their environment, creating dangerously cold conditions for populations that had adapted to warmer circumstances.

Their suffering became known as the Ice Death.

A mysterious figure called the Sovereign helped them survive by introducing magical technology that allowed people to access stored heat.

The Sovereign was later revealed to be Kelsier, not the original Lord Ruler.

Southern societies developed advanced applications of Feruchemy, including medallions that allow people without innate powers to use certain magical abilities.

Their airships and other technologies create new opportunities for cooperation, trade, and conflict with the northern nations.

AUTONOMY AND THE EVENTS OF THE LOST METAL

During Era Two, a secretive organization known as the Set attempts to gain political and technological power.

The Set becomes connected to Autonomy, an external Shard whose influence threatens Scadrial.

Telsin Ladrian, Wax's sister, becomes a central figure in Autonomy's plans.

In The Lost Metal, Wax, Wayne, Marasi, Steris, and their allies work to prevent a catastrophic attack on Elendel.

Wayne ultimately sacrifices his life to prevent an enormous explosion from destroying the city.

The immediate threat is defeated, but Scadrial remains vulnerable to broader conflicts involving other powers in the Cosmere.

Harmony also experiences increasing difficulties balancing Preservation and Ruin.

IMPORTANT LOCATIONS

Luthadel was the capital of the Final Empire and the center of its political and religious authority.

Kredik Shaw was the Lord Ruler's palace, containing the hidden location of the Well of Ascension.

The Pits of Hathsin were a prison and mining complex associated with the production of atium.

The Terris region was the ancestral homeland of the Feruchemical people whose traditions preserved extensive historical knowledge.

Elendel is the principal city of the post-Catacendre northern civilization.

The Roughs are frontier-like territories outside the more densely settled Elendel Basin.

Southern Scadrial is home to distinct nations and traditions whose magical technologies differ from those of the north.

CANON NOTES AND LIMITATIONS

Mistborn Era One and Era Two occur in different historical periods. Their political institutions, available technologies, and common magical abilities should not be treated as identical.

The Sovereign of Southern Scadrial is Kelsier, while the Lord Ruler of the Final Empire was Rashek.

The Catacendre ended the Final Empire's environmental conditions but did not eliminate social inequality or political conflict.

This document covers the completed Era One and Era Two storylines through The Lost Metal. Later planned Mistborn eras are not treated as established history.
