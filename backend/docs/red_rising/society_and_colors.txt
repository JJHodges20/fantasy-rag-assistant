TITLE: Red Rising — Society and Color Hierarchy
SERIES: Red Rising
COVERAGE: Red Rising, Golden Son, Morning Star, Iron Gold, Dark Age, and Light Bringer
SPOILER LEVEL: FULL
CANON BASIS: Pierce Brown's published Red Rising novels through Light Bringer

OVERVIEW

The Society is the authoritarian civilization governing humanity across the solar system at the beginning of Red Rising. Its institutions are built around a rigid hierarchy of genetically differentiated human castes called Colors. Each Color is assigned a social function, while Golds monopolize political leadership and justify their dominance through claims of superior ability and responsibility.

The Society presents its system as essential for order, prosperity, and humanity's expansion into space. In practice, it relies on inherited privilege, slavery, propaganda, military force, and restrictions on personal freedom.

THE ORIGINS OF THE SOCIETY

Humanity expanded beyond Earth, establishing settlements on Luna and eventually across the solar system. The Color system emerged during this period of colonization and became an increasingly rigid form of social organization.

Gold rulers consolidated their power through the Conquering, a military conflict that ended Earth's independence and established Gold supremacy.

Over generations, genetic engineering and controlled reproduction produced significant biological differences between Colors. These differences reinforced the claim that each group possessed a natural place in society.

The Society's rulers used history, education, and cultural conditioning to discourage challenges to the hierarchy.

THE FOURTEEN COLORS

Golds are the ruling caste, including aristocrats, politicians, military commanders, and many of the Society's most powerful warriors. Golds possess exceptional physical advantages and undergo education designed to prepare them for leadership. However, wealth, lineage, and political connections create substantial inequalities even within Gold society.

Silvers dominate banking, commerce, finance, and major business enterprises. Their control over wealth gives them considerable influence, although they remain politically subordinate to Gold.

Whites fulfill specialized religious, ceremonial, and judicial roles. Their appearance and education distinguish them from other Colors, and they often serve powerful institutions.

Coppers are administrators, bureaucrats, legal specialists, and officials who help operate the Society's complicated government.

Blues are trained for spaceflight, navigation, and the operation of sophisticated spacecraft. Their technical abilities are essential to interplanetary travel and warfare.

Yellows specialize in medicine, surgery, and scientific research. Their work supports healthcare and biological technologies.

Greens work with computing, digital systems, programming, and technical communications.

Violets are artists, designers, and specialists in creative fields. Some Violets, called Carvers, perform advanced biological alterations and physical reconstruction.

Oranges are engineers, mechanics, and technical workers responsible for industrial machinery, vehicles, and equipment.

Grays serve primarily as soldiers, police, guards, and security personnel. They form much of the Society's conventional military and law-enforcement infrastructure.

Browns perform domestic and service work, including cooking, cleaning, and household labor.

Pinks are bred and exploited for companionship, entertainment, and sexual servitude. Their treatment is one of the clearest examples of the Society's institutionalized abuse.

Obsidians are physically imposing warriors developed for combat. Gold authorities manipulate many Obsidian communities through religious beliefs and social isolation to maintain their obedience.

Reds perform mining, excavation, and other physically demanding labor. LowReds occupy some of the most oppressed positions in the hierarchy, while HighReds often experience different working conditions.

THE DECEPTION OF THE RED MINERS

Darrow begins the story as a Helldiver in the mining settlement of Lykos, beneath the surface of Mars.

The miners believe they are extracting helium-3 to support the future terraforming of Mars. They regard their dangerous work as a sacrifice that will allow later generations to inhabit the planet.

Darrow eventually discovers that Mars has already been terraformed and populated for generations. The miners have been deliberately deceived and kept underground as an exploited labor force.

This revelation exposes the fundamental dishonesty of the Society's supposedly benevolent system.

GOLD POLITICS AND THE SOVEREIGN

The Society's political structure combines aristocratic families, powerful planetary governors, military leaders, and a supreme ruler called the Sovereign.

At the beginning of the series, Octavia au Lune holds the position of Sovereign, exercising authority from Luna.

Powerful Gold houses compete for prestige, territory, military command, and influence over the government. Rivalries between families frequently result in assassination, betrayal, and warfare.

The Institute is an important institution for training and selecting exceptional Gold leaders. Students compete through brutal simulated warfare, where their decisions determine reputation and future opportunities.

Graduates who earn the Peerless Scar often occupy prestigious positions within Gold civilization.

THE CORE AND THE RIM

The Core encompasses the politically central regions of the solar system, including Luna and the major inner planetary powers.

The Rim comprises more distant settlements and territories, especially the moons of the outer planets.

The Rim's Gold rulers often maintain stronger regional identities and political ambitions distinct from those of the Core.

The Raa family becomes especially important in the Rim, where questions of autonomy, honor, military power, and loyalty repeatedly influence the wider conflicts.

Disagreements between Core and Rim Golds shape several major alliances and betrayals.

THE RISING AND THE SOLAR REPUBLIC

The Sons of Ares organize resistance against Gold supremacy, eventually helping transform Darrow into an infiltrator capable of challenging the Society from within.

The Rising becomes a broad revolutionary movement opposing the Color hierarchy.

Following the defeat of Octavia au Lune in Morning Star, Virginia au Augustus becomes Sovereign of the new Solar Republic.

The Republic attempts to replace inherited caste privilege with a political system founded on broader rights and representation.

However, the transition is difficult. Economic inequality, prejudice, political factionalism, and military threats survive the fall of the old government.

The Republic must also confront Gold loyalists who consider the revolution a disaster and seek to restore the Society.

CANON NOTES AND LIMITATIONS

The Color hierarchy describes the Society's intended structure, not the moral worth or individual capabilities of its members.

Conditions vary between locations, eras, and political regimes. The Solar Republic does not instantly eliminate centuries of discrimination.

The story remains politically unresolved at the end of Light Bringer. This document does not assume an outcome for the final confrontation.
